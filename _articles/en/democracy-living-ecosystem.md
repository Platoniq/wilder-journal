**[Jean-Paul Gagnon](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/persons/jean-paul-gagnon/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)** is a philosopher of democracy at the University of Canberra and co-editor of *Democratic Theory*, whose work has pushed democratic theory beyond its familiar institutional and Western boundaries by investigating democracy’s many linguistic forms, its neglected histories and its possible manifestations beyond the human world. 

In this conversation with **Olivier Schulbaum**, strategic director of Platoniq, Gagnon invites us to think of democracy not as a single concept but as a vast and still largely unexplored ecosystem of meanings: an archive scattered across languages, cultures, artefacts and practices that conventional political theory has often failed to recognise. The discussion moves from the colonial and universalising assumptions embedded in democratic theory to embodied and non-discursive forms of political expression; from the collective decision-making of honeybees to forests understood as multispecies democracies; and from the disappearance of languages as a loss of democratic imagination to the political, ecological and labour conditions underlying artificial intelligence. What emerges is an expansive account of democracy as something that may be spoken, embodied, sensed, shared across species and technologically mediated and a challenge to rethink not only how democracy should work, but where, and in what forms, we are willing to recognise it.
